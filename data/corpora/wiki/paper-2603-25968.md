# Neuro-Cognitive Reward Modeling for Human-Centered Autonomous Vehicle Control

**Authors:** Zhuoli Zhuang, Yu-Cheng Chang, Yu-Kai Wang, Thomas Do, Chin-Teng Lin  
**ArXiv:** 2603.25968  
**Year:** 2026

## Summary

This paper introduces an EEG-guided decision-making framework that incorporates human cognitive insights into [[rlhf]] for autonomous driving without requiring behavioral response interruption. Using event-related potentials (ERPs) from 20 participants in a VR driving simulator, the authors develop a cognitive reward model that predicts EEG features from visual scenes and integrates them into RL training.

## Core Innovation

### EEG-Based Human Feedback
- **Non-Invasive Monitoring**: Electroencephalography captures neural activity without interrupting driving
- **Event-Related Potentials**: Time-locked responses to cognitive/sensory events (300-500ms P3 component)
- **Natural Responses**: Millisecond-level temporal resolution of brain activity
- **Scalable Training**: No EEG data required during inference, unlike traditional brain-computer interfaces

### Cognitive Reward Framework
1. **EEG Data Collection**: 20 participants in realistic VR driving simulator
2. **ERP Analysis**: Extract prominent features correlated with driver reaction time
3. **Prediction Model**: Neural network predicts ERP strength from scene images
4. **RL Integration**: Cognitive predictions incorporated into reward signal

## Experimental Setup

### Data Collection
- **Participants**: 20 healthy drivers (mean age 25.6±4.6 years, 4.9±3.9 years driving experience)
- **Equipment**: HTC Vive Pro Eye VR headset, Logitech G923 racing wheel, 64-channel EEG (Synamps2)
- **Scenarios**: Emergency braking and adaptive cruise control tasks
- **Multimodal Data**: EEG signals, eye-tracking, behavioral responses, scene images

### ERP Analysis
- **P3 Component**: Positive peak 300-500ms after unexpected stimulus onset
- **Oddball Paradigm**: Larger amplitude for infrequent, unexpected events
- **Task Difficulty**: ERP amplitude increases with cognitive load
- **Covert Attention**: Detects focus outside fovea (complementary to eye-tracking)

## Technical Approach

### Human Cognitive Reward Model
- **Input**: Scene images from driving simulator
- **Output**: Predicted ERP strength indicating cognitive surprise/difficulty
- **Architecture**: Neural network trained on participant EEG responses
- **Integration**: Prediction probability used as component of RL reward signal

### RLHF Enhancement
Advantages over traditional preference collection:
- **Efficiency**: No manual ranking of trajectory segments
- **Naturalness**: Captures involuntary cognitive responses
- **Temporal Precision**: Millisecond-level feedback timing
- **Objectivity**: Reduces subjective bias in preference annotation

## Experimental Results

### Collision Avoidance Improvement
- **Enhanced Performance**: RL algorithm shows improved collision avoidance
- **Two Complex Scenarios**: Emergency braking and adaptive cruise control
- **Cognitive Alignment**: Better correspondence with human driver expectations
- **Reaction Time Correlation**: ERP amplitude correlates with driver response latency

### Validation Findings
- **ERP-Behavior Link**: Significant correlation between neural responses and driving performance
- **Predictive Accuracy**: Scene-based ERP prediction enables effective reward shaping
- **Scalability**: Framework works without real-time EEG during deployment
