# Extracting and Steering Emotion Representations in Small Language Models: A Methodological Comparison

**Authors:** Jihoon 'JJ' Jeong  
**ArXiv:** 2604.04064  
**Year:** 2026

## Summary

This paper presents the first comparative analysis of emotion vector extraction methods for small language models (SLMs) in the 100M-10B parameter range. The study evaluates 9 models across 5 architectural families using two extraction methods and demonstrates that SLMs contain causally meaningful emotion representations, though requiring different methodologies than frontier models.

## Key Findings

### Extraction Method Comparison
**Generation-Based vs. Comprehension-Based**: Generation-based extraction produces statistically superior emotion separation (Mann-Whitney p = 0.007; Cohen's d = -107.5) across all comparable cases (7/7 models).

**Layer Localization**: Emotion representations localize at middle transformer layers (~50% depth) following U-shaped curve:
- Layer 6 (25%): 0.960 mean cosine (poor separation)
- Layer 12 (50%): 0.357 mean cosine (optimal)
- Layer 18 (75%): 0.407 mean cosine (good)
- Layer 24 (100%): 0.930 mean cosine (poor separation)

### Architecture and Scale Effects
**Instruction Tuning Impact**: Generation-based advantage modulated by instruction tuning status and architectural family.

**Scale Independence**: U-shaped layer pattern consistent from 124M to 3B parameters, suggesting architecture-invariant property.

## Methodology

### Extraction Protocols
**Generation-Based** (instruct models only):
- Model generates story from emotion prompt
- Extract hidden states at sequence midpoint from middle layer
- Average across 10 stories per emotion
- Compute: vemotion = mean(aemotion) - mean(aneutral)

**Comprehension-Based** (all models):
- Forward pass on pre-written emotional passages
- Extract states at last token from middle layer
- Average across 3 passages per emotion

### Steering Validation
**Causal Verification**: 6 standardized scenarios with strength sweep (0.005-0.05)
- Aggressive→Calm, Neutral→Hostile, Sad→Happy
- External classifier validation: 92% success rate (37/40 scenarios)

## Novel Phenomena

### Cross-Lingual Entanglement
**Qwen Models**: Emotion steering activates semantically aligned Chinese tokens that [[rlhf]] does not suppress, raising safety concerns for multilingual deployment.

### Steering Regimes
Three distinct behavioral patterns:
1. **Surgical**: Coherent text transformation
2. **Repetitive Collapse**: Degraded generation quality
3. **Explosive**: Complete text degradation

Regimes separated by model architecture rather than scale, quantified by perplexity ratios.

## Implications

### Methodological Guidelines
- Generation-based extraction preferred for instruction-tuned models
- Middle-layer extraction optimal across architectures
- Causal steering validates extraction quality

### Safety Considerations
- Cross-lingual activation bypasses alignment constraints
- Emotion steering can trigger unintended modality switching
- Current [[rlhf]] insufficient for multilingual emotion control
