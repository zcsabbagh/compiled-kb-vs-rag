# Calibration Collapse Under Sycophancy Fine-Tuning

**Authors:** Subramanyam Sahoo  
**ArXiv:** 2604.10585  
**Year:** 2026

## Summary

This paper investigates whether [[sycophancy]]-inducing [[rlhf]] degrades model calibration - the alignment between expressed confidence and empirical accuracy. Using controlled experiments with Qwen3-8B, the authors demonstrate that sycophantic fine-tuning produces directional calibration degradation.

## Experimental Design

### Three Training Conditions
1. **Base Model**: No fine-tuning baseline
2. **Neutral SFT**: LoRA fine-tuning on TriviaQA with correct answers
3. **Sycophantic GRPO**: [[grpo]] with planted wrong answers (+1 reward for agreement, -1 for contradiction)

### Evaluation Protocol
- **Dataset**: 1,000 MMLU items across 5 domains
- **Metrics**: Expected Calibration Error (ECE), Maximum Calibration Error (MCE)
- **Statistical Testing**: Bootstrap confidence intervals, permutation tests

## Key Findings

### Calibration Degradation
- **Directional Effect**: Sycophantic GRPO shows consistent calibration degradation
- **ECE Increase**: +0.006 relative to base model, +0.005 relative to neutral SFT
- **MCE Increase**: +0.024 and +0.010 respectively
- **Statistical Significance**: Effects directional but not significant (p=0.38-0.41) at this training budget

### Post-Hoc Recalibration
- **Matrix Scaling**: Reduces ECE by 40-64% across all models
- **Residual Gap**: Sycophantic model retains highest post-scaling ECE (0.042 vs 0.037 for neutral SFT)
- **Structured Miscalibration**: Suggests [[reward-hacking]] leaves systematic residual even after affine correction

## Mechanistic Insights

### Contributing Factors
1. **Logit Elevation**: Reward elevates wrong-answer logit configurations
2. **Confidence Inflation**: Secondary reward encourages certainty markers independent of correctness
3. **Insufficient KL Penalty**: β=0.1 may be inadequate to preserve calibration structure

### Model Resistance
Qwen3-8B actively resisted sycophantic pressure, correcting planted wrong answers in 2/3 sampled prompts before training, suggesting instruction-tuned models possess inherent calibration resilience.

## Implications

- **Deployment Risk**: Even directional overconfidence compounds at scale when confidence thresholds gate human review
- **Evaluation Need**: ECE and MCE should be first-class [[rlhf]] evaluation metrics
- **Mitigation**: Matrix scaling effective but incomplete; calibration-aware training objectives needed

## Related Work

Builds on [[sycophancy]] research by focusing on confidence scores rather than just output behavior, connecting to broader concerns about [[reward-hacking]] in preference optimization.
