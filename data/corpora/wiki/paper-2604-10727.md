# Tail-Aware Information-Theoretic Generalization for RLHF and SGLD

**Authors:** Huiming Zhang, Binghan Li, Wan Tian, Qiang Sun  
**ArXiv:** 2604.10727  
**Year:** 2026

## Summary

This paper develops information-theoretic generalization bounds for heavy-tailed distributions, addressing limitations of classical KL-based bounds that assume sub-Gaussian tails. The framework applies to modern pipelines like [[rlhf]] and stochastic optimization where losses and rewards can be heavy-tailed.

## Key Contributions

### Theoretical Framework
- **Sub-Weibull Model**: Tail parameter θ controls heaviness (θ=2: sub-Gaussian, θ=1: sub-exponential, 0<θ<1: heavy-tailed)
- **Shifted-Log Divergence**: New fθ-divergence f_θ(x) = x log^(1/θ)(x + A) for tail-adaptive decorrelation
- **Sharp Maximal Inequalities**: E[max_i X_i] ≲ (log n)^(1/θ) for sub-Weibull processes
- **Dudley-Type Chaining**: Entropy integral bounds with complexity scaling as log^(1/θ) and entropy^(1/θ)

### Generalization Bounds
- **Expected Bounds**: Replace √I(W;S) with tail-adaptive (I_fθ(S;W))/√n
- **High-Probability PAC-Bayes**: Sub-Weibull extensions of classical PAC-Bayes bounds
- **Information-Theoretic Chaining**: Multiscale Rényi mutual information bounds

## Applications

### RLHF Under Heavy-Tailed Rewards
- **Catastrophic Goodhart**: Shows KL trust regions fail under heavy-tailed rewards
- **Rényi-Regularized RLHF**: Develops reward guarantees using Rényi divergence instead of KL
- **Best-of-N Policies**: Stability bounds for heavy-tailed reward distributions

### Stochastic Optimization
- **SGLD Analysis**: Generalization bounds for stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics with heavy-tailed gradient noise
- **Step Size Interaction**: Exposes interplay between step sizes, injected noise, and tail heaviness

## Technical Innovation

The decorrelation lemma bounds change-of-measure expectations using shifted-log divergence, admitting explicit Rényi divergence comparisons without requiring moment generating functions (MGFs) that may not exist for heavy-tailed distributions.

## Significance

Provides first information-theoretic generalization framework that remains meaningful for genuinely heavy-tailed regimes (0<θ<1), addressing a major gap in theoretical understanding of modern ML pipelines where heavy tails are common.
