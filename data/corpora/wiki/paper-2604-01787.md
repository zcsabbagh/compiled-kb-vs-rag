# DEFT: Distribution-guided Efficient Fine-Tuning for Human Alignment

**Authors:** Liang Zhu, Feiteng Fang, Yuelin Bai, Longze Chen, Zhexiang Zhang, Minghuan Tan, Min Yang  
**ArXiv:** 2604.01787  
**Year:** 2026

## Summary

This paper introduces Distribution-guided Efficient Fine-Tuning (DEFT), an alignment framework that enhances existing methods through data filtering and distributional guidance. DEFT calculates differential distribution rewards based on model output distributions and preference data discrepancies to achieve more efficient alignment with reduced training costs.

## Core Innovation

### Discrepancy Distribution
DEFT extracts positive and negative token distributions from preference data:
- **Positive Distribution Q+**: Aggregated from chosen responses
- **Negative Distribution Q-**: Aggregated from rejected responses  
- **Discrepancy Distribution**: Qdiff = Q+ - Q- (after normalization)

This subtraction amplifies preference-relevant information while eliminating redundant natural language content.

### Distribution Reward
Calculates reward based on KL divergence between model output distribution and discrepancy distribution:
- **Data Filtering**: Selects high-quality subset from raw preference data
- **Training Guidance**: Incorporates distribution reward into existing alignment methods
- **Efficiency**: Reduces training time while improving performance

## Technical Approach

### Integration with Existing Methods
DEFT enhances both:
- **PRO (Preference Ranking Optimization)**: SFT extension with contrastive learning
- **DPO (Direct Preference Optimization)**: Direct policy optimization from preferences

### Two-Stage Process
1. **Filtering Stage**: Use distribution reward to select informative preference pairs
2. **Training Stage**: Guide model output distribution toward discrepancy distribution

## Experimental Results

### Performance Improvements
- **Better Alignment**: Superior preference learning outcomes across benchmarks
- **Reduced Training Cost**: Significantly fewer training steps and time required
- **Enhanced Generalization**: Retained or improved capabilities on general tasks
- **Efficiency**: Small high-quality subsets outperform full datasets

### Validation
Comprehensive experiments demonstrate DEFT-enhanced methods consistently outperform original approaches in both alignment capability and generalization ability.

## Implications

### Alignment Efficiency
Addresses the "alignment tax" problem by showing that:
- Quality matters more than quantity in preference data
- Distribution-level guidance improves learning efficiency
- Proper data filtering prevents overfitting to low-quality examples

### Theoretical Foundation
Supports the "superficial alignment hypothesis" by focusing on output distribution alignment rather than deep behavioral changes.
