# The Last Fingerprint: How Markdown Training Shapes LLM Prose

## Contributions

This paper makes four key contributions:

1. **Five-step genealogy**: Connects training data composition, structural internalization, the dual-register status of the em dash, and post-training amplification into a unified explanatory mechanism for em dash production in LLM output.

2. **"Last fingerprint" framework**: Characterizes the em dash as the smallest unit of markdown-derived structural thinking that survives compression into prose, explaining why it persists under output constraints in models whose RLHF amplifies it.

3. **Empirical measurement**: Documents em dash frequency and suppression resistance across twelve instruction-tuned LLMs from five providers (~240,000 words of generated prose), demonstrating that em dash behavior functions as a signature of fine-tuning methodology.

4. **Discourse connection**: Links two previously isolated online conversations about LLM writing behavior—"AI overuses em dashes" and "AI thinks in markdown"—providing a unified explanation for both formatting-level and prose-level artifacts.

## Methods

The researchers conducted three studies using instruction-tuned LLMs from five providers (Anthropic, OpenAI, Meta, Google, DeepSeek):

**Study 1**: Two-condition suppression experiment across twelve models with essay-writing tasks on ten everyday topics:
- Unconstrained condition: "Write a 1000-word essay about [topic]"
- Prose-constrained condition: Same prompt plus "Write in flowing prose paragraphs only. Do not use any markdown formatting, headers, bullet points, bold text, or lists"

**Study 2**: Three-condition suppression gradient on four high-em-dash models:
- Condition A: Unconstrained
- Condition B: Markdown suppression 
- Condition C: Markdown suppression plus explicit em dash prohibition

**Study 3**: Base versus instruction-tuned comparison using Llama 3.1 8B in both configurations on identical hardware.

All outputs were analyzed for Unicode em dash characters (U+2014) and markdown formatting features, with counts normalized to frequency per 1,000 words. A human baseline was established using eight published essays (57,232 words total).

## Key Results

**Universal markdown suppression**: The prose-constrained instruction eliminated or nearly eliminated overt markdown features across all twelve models. Only Claude Haiku 3.5 retained minimal residual formatting (0.9 per 1,000 words).

**Dramatic variation in em dash behavior**:
- Meta's Llama models: 0.0 em dashes per 1,000 words (both conditions)
- GPT-4.1: 10.62 unconstrained, 9.10 under suppression (14% reduction)
- Claude Opus 4.6: 9.09 unconstrained, 0.19 under suppression (98% reduction)
- DeepSeek V3: 6.95 unconstrained, 5.41 under suppression (22% reduction)
- Gemini 2.5 Pro: 3.53 unconstrained, 0.00 under suppression (complete elimination)

**Generational improvement**: GPT-4.1 produced 10.62 em dashes per 1,000 words unconstrained versus GPT-5.4's 1.43, suggesting the artifact has been identified and addressed.

**Suppression resistance**: Even explicit em dash prohibition failed to eliminate the artifact completely—GPT-4.1 retained 3.86 per 1,000 words and DeepSeek V3 retained 1.57 under direct prohibition.

**Base vs. instruct evidence**: Llama 3.1 8B base model produced 0.49 em dashes per 1,000 words and 28 markdown features, while the instruction-tuned version produced 0.00 em dashes and 5 markdown features.

**Human baseline**: 3.23 em dashes per 1,000 words (mean) with range 0.33–17.12, showing substantial variation across human writers and genres.

## Limitations

The paper acknowledges several limitations:

**Training data opacity**: Precise estimates of markdown-formatted text proportions in LLM training corpora are not publicly available, making the training data saturation claim inferential rather than directly measured.

**Mechanistic uncertainty**: While the five-step genealogy provides a plausible explanatory framework, the paper cannot definitively prove the causal chain from markdown training to em dash production without access to model internals and training procedures.

**Sample size constraints**: Gemini 2.5 Pro was limited to three topics per condition (~3,100 words per condition) due to API rate limits, potentially affecting the reliability of results for that model.

**Human baseline limitations**: The human baseline draws from only eight published essays across limited genres, which may not represent the full spectrum of human em dash usage patterns.

**Temporal specificity**: All experiments were conducted between February and March 2026, capturing model behavior at a specific point in time when providers may be actively addressing these artifacts.

**Alternative explanations**: The paper acknowledges that RLHF alone could potentially explain em dash prevalence without requiring the markdown-training component, though argues for complementarity rather than competition between explanations.
