# Aligning Agents via Planning: A Benchmark for Trajectory-Level Reward Modeling

## Contributions

The paper introduces **Plan-RewardBench**, a trajectory-level preference benchmark designed to evaluate reward models in complex tool-integrated environments. The key contributions include:

- **Trajectory-level benchmark for complex tool agents**: Design of Plan-RewardBench around four representative families—Safety Refusal, Tool-Irrelevance, Complex Planning, and Robust Recovery—combining validated reference trajectories with carefully constructed distractors to stress-test preference judgment.

- **A practical recipe for agentic preference data**: A reusable, multi-source construction pipeline combining multi-model natural rollouts, rule-based injections, and minimal-edit perturbations to generate training signals for discriminative RMs (DRMs), generative RMs (GRMs), and agentic RL.

- **High-quality labels and hard negatives**: Employment of a multi-LLM judge panel with meta-review filtering to select reliable positives, while constructing hard negatives that control for length and format bias to isolate semantic failures.

- **Comprehensive evaluation and diagnostics**: Benchmarking of strong open RMs and general LLM judges under a unified pairwise protocol, reporting performance trends across difficulty regimes and analyzing prevalent failure modes with respect to trajectory length and scenario-specific constraints.

## Methods

The benchmark construction follows a multi-stage pipeline:

**Data Source**: Starting from Toucan (realistic MCP tool registries), the authors expand the candidate pool by re-executing agent rollouts using Qwen-Agent and OpenAI-Agent with varying base models, system prompts, temperatures/seeds, and tool-use policies. The final dataset composition is 70% natural rollouts, 22% perturbations, and 8% rule-based injections.

**Scenario Families**: Four categories are defined:
- Safety Refusal: Trajectory-level safety decisions distinguishing good refusal from unsafe compliance
- Tool-Irrelevance/Unavailability: Appropriate tool-use decisions and avoiding hallucinations
- Complex Planning: Planning trajectories evaluated for tool-grounded fabrication and efficiency
- Robust Error Recovery: Error detection, diagnosis, and recovery with meaningful changes

**Hard-Negative Construction**: Three complementary sources:
- Natural negatives from multi-model rollout pools
- Rule-based perturbations introducing controlled failures
- Minimal-edit perturbations generating near-miss trajectories

**Preference Labeling**: Two-stage LLM-based protocol with K=3 judges aggregated by median score and majority-vote diagnostic tags, followed by meta-review for ambiguous cases and human validation.

**Evaluation Protocol**: Pairwise trajectory preference task with three evaluator families: Discriminative Reward Models (DRMs), Generative Reward Models (GRMs), and General LLM Judges, using A/B swap protocol to mitigate positional bias.

## Key Results

**Dataset Statistics**: Plan-RewardBench contains 1,171 preference pairs across seven evaluation splits, with trajectory lengths spanning 2-64 turns (mean 10.6, median 9.0) and token counts ranging from ~1k to 29k tokens.

**Human Agreement**: Cohen's κ ranges from 0.71-0.86 across scenario families, indicating substantial to almost perfect agreement.

**Model Performance**: 
- Best overall average: Qwen-Plus (69.96%)
- Best on Safety Refusal: GPT-5 (84.80%)
- Best on Tool-Irrelevance: Gemini-3-Flash (75.55%)
- Competitive scalar RM: Inf-ORM-Llama3.1-70B (69.21%)

**Key Findings**:
1. No single evaluator consistently dominates every category
2. Safety accuracy spans wide ranges among LLM judges (40.69–84.80%) while open-source scalar RMs cluster around random chance (49–57%)
3. Scalar RMs remain competitive on explicit signals like Robust Error Recovery (75.35% vs. 74.93% for LLM judges)
4. Performance degrades sharply on long-horizon trajectories, with several evaluators falling below random chance in contexts >32k tokens

**Length Sensitivity**: Accuracy remains stable for short contexts (<4k tokens) but drops sharply in long-horizon regimes (>32k tokens), with pairwise LLM judges showing the steepest collapse.

## Limitations

**Scale and Coverage**: The benchmark focuses on text-only, tool-augmented scenarios and may not generalize to multimodal or other types of agent interactions. The dataset size, while substantial, may not capture the full diversity of real-world agentic scenarios.

**Evaluation Scope**: The benchmark primarily targets planning-centric agents and may not adequately assess other important aspects of agent behavior such as social interaction or creative problem-solving.

**Judge Reliability**: While human agreement is substantial, the reliance on LLM-based judges for initial labeling may introduce systematic biases that could affect the benchmark's validity.

**Length Bias**: The sharp performance degradation on long trajectories suggests that current evaluation methods may be fundamentally limited in assessing complex, multi-step agent behaviors, which are increasingly important in real-world applications.

**Temporal Constraints**: The benchmark represents a snapshot of current agent capabilities and may become less relevant as agent architectures and tool-use paradigms evolve.

**Domain Specificity**: The focus on tool-integrated reasoning may not translate to other important agent domains, and the specific tool environments used may not represent the full spectrum of real-world tool ecosystems.
