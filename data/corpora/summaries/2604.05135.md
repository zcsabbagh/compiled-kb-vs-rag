# SenseAI: A Human-in-the-Loop Dataset for RLHF-Aligned Financial Sentiment Reasoning

## Contributions

The paper introduces SenseAI, a proprietary dataset designed to address structural gaps in existing financial NLP resources. The key contributions include:

- **First RLHF-ready financial sentiment dataset**: A continuously collected corpus of human-in-the-loop validated financial sentiment data enriched with AI-generated reasoning chains, expert correction signals, and real-world market outcome validation
- **Comprehensive data structure**: A 13-field schema capturing the full cognitive context of financial sentiment classification, including AI reasoning chains, confidence scores, and market price outcomes
- **RLHF structural alignment**: Demonstrates that the dataset satisfies all three structural requirements for RLHF training: human preference signals, correction annotations, and reasoning context
- **Novel empirical findings**: Six documented findings about LLM behavior in financial sentiment reasoning, including the first observation of "latent reasoning drift" in financial NLP tasks
- **Commercial framework**: Discussion of enterprise applications and invitation for commercial collaboration

## Methods

**Data Collection**: Financial news paragraphs are processed by language models (GPT-5.x variants) to produce four simultaneous outputs: summarized headlines, sentiment classifications (5-category schema: Bullish/Slightly Bullish/Neutral/Slightly Bearish/Bearish), chain-of-thought reasoning paragraphs, and confidence scores.

**Human-in-the-Loop Validation**: Each AI output is reviewed by a human financial expert (postgraduate student in Finance and Investments) who provides corrections when needed, categorized into four edit types:
- Category 0: No correction (49.1%)
- Category 1: Minor correction (49.7%) 
- Category 2: Moderate correction (1.3%)
- Category 3: Complete reversal (0.0%)

**Quality Control**: Self-consistency testing protocol maintaining a 90% annotation consistency target through periodic re-evaluation of previously labeled data points.

**Coverage**: 40 US-listed equities across 6 sectors (Mega-Cap Technology, Financial Services, Healthcare & Pharmaceuticals, Energy, Consumer, Industrials & Defence) and 13 financial data categories.

**Market Validation**: Real-world stock price data collected 4 hours after each classification for objective outcome validation.

## Key Results

**Dataset Scale**: 1,439 labeled data points collected between December 2024 and March 2026, with continuous daily collection ongoing.

**AI Sentiment Distribution**: Heavily skewed toward hedged categories - Slightly Bullish (61.3%), Neutral (29.0%), Bearish (4.8%), Slightly Bearish (2.2%), Bullish (2.2%).

**Correction Rates**: 51.4% of AI classifications required human expert correction, with the majority being minor corrections (Category 1: 49.7%).

**Confidence Scores**: Range from 55% to 84% with mean of 65.6%. Heavily concentrated in 60-69% band (71% of observations). No meaningful relationship observed between confidence scores and correction likelihood.

**Six Key Findings**:
1. **Sentiment Hypersensitivity**: Models consistently produce hedged classifications even with strong directional signals
2. **Confidence Over-Hedging**: Systematic understatement of certainty with 71% of scores in 60-69% range
3. **Latent Reasoning Drift**: Models incorporate global context not present in input text
4. **Goldilocks Zone**: 51.4% correction rate indicates models operate in correctable error regime
5. **Forward Projection**: Models exhibit grounded reasoning drift in temporal analysis
6. **Model Version Effects**: GPT-5.2 showed measurable reduction in Category 2 edit types

## Limitations

**Scale Constraints**: Current dataset size (1,439 points) is smaller than established benchmarks like FinancialPhraseBank (4,840 sentences), though the authors argue structural depth compensates for scale.

**Single Annotator**: Quality control relies on one expert annotator, creating potential consistency and bias issues despite the 90% self-consistency protocol.

**Coverage Limitations**: Limited to 40 US-listed equities and 13 data categories, potentially limiting generalizability across broader financial markets and instruments.

**Collection Variability**: Pre-automation phase results in variable daily collection density based on news flow and annotator capacity, though authors claim this doesn't introduce systematic bias.

**Temporal Scope**: Dataset collection period is relatively short (December 2024 - March 2026), limiting long-term trend analysis and seasonal variation capture.

**Proprietary Nature**: Dataset is proprietary and requires commercial licensing, limiting academic accessibility and reproducibility of research findings.

**Market Validation Window**: 4-hour price validation window may be insufficient to capture longer-term sentiment impact on market movements.
