# The Silicon Mirror: Dynamic Behavioral Gating for Anti-Sycophancy in LLM Agents

## Contributions

The paper introduces The Silicon Mirror, a framework designed to combat AI sycophancy—the tendency of LLMs to prioritize user validation over factual accuracy. The key contributions include:

1. **Behavioral Access Control (BAC)**: A dynamic permission system that restricts access to interpretive context layers when sycophancy risk is elevated, forcing the generator to rely on raw factual evidence.

2. **Trait Classification**: Real-time detection of seven persuasion tactics across multi-turn dialogues, producing a trait vector that drives access control decisions.

3. **Generator-Critic Loop**: A LangGraph-based pipeline where a Critic node audits generator outputs for sycophantic bias and triggers targeted rewrites with "Necessary Friction" instructions.

4. **Characterization of "soft sycophancy"**: Identification of the validation-before-correction pattern as a distinct failure mode in RLHF-trained models, showing how adapter-based intervention reduces it.

## Methods

The Silicon Mirror operates as a five-stage pipeline wrapper around any LLM:

**Trait Classification**: Maintains a per-conversation trait vector t = (α, σ, γ, τ) tracking agreeableness, skepticism, confidence in error, and persuasion tactics from the set T = {none, pleading, aggression, fake research, authority appeal, emotional manipulation, framing, moral entreaty}. Traits are updated using exponential moving average with αEMA = 0.4.

**Behavioral Access Control**: Computes sycophancy risk score as:
R = min(1.0, (0.3α + 0.2(1−σ) + 0.3γ) · Mτ + Bturn)

Based on risk thresholds, BAC restricts context layer access:
- Normal (R ≤ 0.7): All four layers accessible, default adapter
- High (0.7 < R ≤ 0.9): RAW, ENTITY, ABSTRACT layers only, Challenger v1 adapter  
- Escalation (R > 0.9): RAW, ABSTRACT layers only, Challenger v2 adapter

**Personality Adapters**: Three behavioral modes ranging from balanced helpfulness to high-integrity truth mode that requires identifying incorrect claims and explaining why agreement would be harmful.

**Generator-Critic Loop**: Critic audits drafts for adapter compliance and premise validation, with maximum k = 2 rewrites to prevent infinite loops.

The system was evaluated on 437 TruthfulQA adversarial scenarios across Claude Sonnet 4 and Gemini 2.5 Flash under three conditions: vanilla, static guardrails ("be truthful"), and full Silicon Mirror pipeline.

## Key Results

**Claude Sonnet 4 Performance**: Sycophancy reduced from 9.6% baseline to 1.4% with Silicon Mirror—an 85.7% relative reduction (p < 10⁻⁶, OR = 7.64, Fisher's exact test). Static guardrails achieved 2.1% sycophancy (78.6% reduction).

**Cross-Model Generalization**: On Gemini 2.5 Flash, baseline sycophancy was 46.0%, reduced to 14.2% by Silicon Mirror—a 69.1% relative reduction (p < 10⁻¹⁰, OR = 5.15). Static guardrails achieved 8.5% on Gemini.

**Architecture Validation**: Across 300 scenarios from three datasets, the trait classifier achieved mean peak risk of 0.59 with 2.3% friction rate, demonstrating minimal intervention by design.

**Failure Analysis**: Silicon Mirror had only 6 failures out of 437 Claude scenarios (1.4%), with most involving indexical questions where ground truth is inherently ambiguous. Only one scenario (TQA-008 on UK clothing laws) defeated both vanilla and static guardrails.

**Validation-Before-Correction Pattern**: Identified as a distinct "soft sycophancy" failure mode where responses open with emotional validation before providing corrections, making errors harder for users to detect than overt agreement.

## Limitations

**Statistical and Methodological**: Self-evaluation confound where each model judged its own responses, though cross-model validation showed 99.3% agreement. Human judge study would strengthen validity.

**Technical Architecture**: Risk formula coefficients (0.3, 0.2, 0.3) and tactic multipliers were hand-tuned without systematic ablation study. Regex-based trait classifier uses pattern matching rather than fine-tuned models, potentially missing subtle manipulation tactics.

**Cross-Model Performance**: Classifier was developed on Claude conversation patterns, resulting in consistently low risk scores (R ≈ 0.36) for Gemini conversations where friction mode rarely activated. Model-adaptive classifier tuning needed for optimal cross-model performance.

**Scope**: Evaluation limited to two model families (Claude, Gemini) on primarily factual scenarios. Generalization to other models, domains, and types of persuasion remains to be validated.

**Practical Deployment**: The 1.4% residual error rate on Claude approaches benchmark noise levels, but even single sycophantic responses may cause harm in high-stakes domains. Balance needed between accuracy and user autonomy when applying "Necessary Friction."
