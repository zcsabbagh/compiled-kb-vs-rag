# Distributionally Robust Token Optimization in RLHF

## Contributions

This paper introduces **Distributionally Robust Token Optimization (DRTO)**, which combines token-level Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) with Distributionally Robust Optimization (DRO) to improve model robustness under distribution shifts. The key contributions are:

- **First application of DRO at minibatch level within token-level RLHF**: DRTO constructs f-divergence ambiguity sets over trajectory samples and derives robust objectives for actor loss updates, inheriting reasoning advantages of token-level alignment while significantly improving robustness.

- **Two practical DRO realizations**: KL-DRTO uses entropic objectives that emphasize high-loss trajectories through exponential tilting, while χ²-DRTO provides a tractable surrogate based on minibatch mean and standard deviation.

- **Empirical improvements with minimal overhead**: Both methods use the same training pipeline as standard RTO with little to no additional runtime or compute cost, achieving up to +11.83% relative accuracy improvement over standard RTO across benchmarks.

## Methods

DRTO extends Reinforced Token Optimization (RTO) by replacing the standard minibatch loss with a distributionally robust objective. The approach models distribution shift through adversarial reweighting over trajectories in a minibatch.

**Core Framework**: Given minibatch B with uniform distribution P_B, DRTO optimizes:
```
R(θ; ρ) = sup_{Q∈Ω(P_B)} E_{τ_i~Q}[L_i(θ)]
```

**KL-DRTO**: Uses KL-divergence ambiguity sets Ω^{KL}_ρ(P_B) = {Q : D_{KL}(Q||P_B) ≤ ρ}. The robust objective becomes an entropic risk measure:
```
Ψ_η(θ) = (1/η) log(1/n ∑_{i=1}^n e^{ηL_i(θ)})
```
with optimal reweighting Q*(i) = exp(ηL_i(θ))/∑_j exp(ηL_j(θ)).

**χ²-DRTO**: Uses Pearson χ²-divergence ambiguity sets, yielding a tractable upper bound:
```
R_{χ²}(θ; ρ) ≤ L̄(θ) + σ(θ)√ρ
```
where L̄(θ) is the minibatch mean loss and σ(θ) is the standard deviation.

Both methods integrate seamlessly into existing RTO pipelines by modifying only the loss computation step.

## Key Results

**Robustness Improvements**: DRTO demonstrates consistent improvements across five mathematical reasoning benchmarks under various distribution shifts:
- **GSM8K**: χ²-DRTO achieves 9.17% improvement, KL-DRTO achieves 7.24% improvement over RTO
- **MathQA**: χ²-DRTO improves by 1.81%, KL-DRTO by 2.49%
- **GSM(CoT)**: χ²-DRTO shows 11.83% improvement, KL-DRTO shows 6.99% improvement
- **GSM-Plus**: χ²-DRTO improves by 7.92%, KL-DRTO by 6.04%
- **GSM(DE)**: χ²-DRTO achieves 7.89% improvement, KL-DRTO achieves 8.55% improvement

**Training Dynamics**: DRTO variants demonstrate superior optimization characteristics:
- KL-DRTO achieves highest training returns with clear upward trends
- Both variants converge to noticeably lower critic loss than baselines
- DRTO maintains lower PTX loss, indicating less drift from pretraining distribution

**In-Distribution Performance**: DRTO remains competitive on in-distribution evaluation (GSM8K train/test split), with KL-DRTO achieving 0.801 and χ²-DRTO achieving 0.798 accuracy compared to RTO's 0.802.

**Ablation Studies**: Comparison with DRPPO variants confirms that token-wise rewards remain essential - DRTO consistently outperforms robust PPO variants across all benchmarks.

## Limitations

**Hyperparameter Sensitivity**: Performance varies across different ambiguity radius values (ρ), requiring grid search to find optimal settings. Different benchmarks peak at different radii, suggesting task-specific tuning may be beneficial.

**Modest Gains on Some Benchmarks**: Improvements on MathQA are more limited compared to other benchmarks, indicating the approach may be less effective for certain types of distribution shifts or problem domains.

**Limited Scope of Evaluation**: Experiments focus primarily on mathematical reasoning tasks with a single model architecture (Llama3-8B). Generalization to other domains and model sizes remains to be demonstrated.

**Theoretical Gaps**: While the paper provides theoretical guarantees for the DRO formulation, the connection between minibatch-level robustness and actual deployment-time distribution shifts could be made more explicit.

**Computational Overhead**: Although claimed to have minimal overhead, the method requires additional computations for ambiguity set optimization and reweighting, which may accumulate over large-scale training.
