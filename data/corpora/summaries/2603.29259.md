# Aligning Multimodal Sequential Recommendations via Robust Direct Preference Optimization with Sparse MoE

## Contributions

The paper makes three key contributions to multimodal sequential recommendation:

1. **Identifies the false negative problem**: The authors identify false negatives as the primary source of DPO brittleness under implicit feedback in recommendation systems, where unobserved items are not necessarily disliked but simply unexposed.

2. **Proposes Stochastic Top-K Negative Sampling**: A simple plug-and-play modification to DPO that replaces deterministic hard negatives with stochastic sampling from a dynamic top-K candidate pool, balancing gradient intensity with false-negative tolerance.

3. **Develops RoDPO framework**: A complete framework for multimodal sequential recommendation that incorporates robust DPO with optional sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) for efficient capacity scaling and a two-stage training protocol.

## Methods

**Problem Setup**: The method addresses multimodal sequential recommendation where users have interaction sequences with items containing multimodal attributes (IDs, images, text, timestamps). The goal is predicting the next item given context.

**Architecture**: 
- Multimodal encoder with modality-specific Transformers for item IDs, text, and images
- Optional sparse Noisy MoE layer with top-k expert selection for capacity scaling
- Temporal fusion module combining modality-specific representations

**Core Innovation - Stochastic Top-K Negative Sampling**:
- Constructs dynamic candidate pool: CK(x) = TopK({sθ(x,i) | i ∈ I \ {yw}}, K)
- Samples losing item uniformly: yl ~ U(CK(x))
- Balances informative hardness with false-negative tolerance

**Training Protocol**:
- Stage 1: Warm-up with standard cross-entropy loss for 15 epochs
- Stage 2: Joint optimization with Ltotal = LCE + λLDPO
- DPO objective: LDPO = -log σ(β(Δsθ - Δsref)) where Δs = s(x,yw) - s(x,yl)

## Key Results

**Overall Performance**: RoDPO consistently outperforms all baselines across three Amazon datasets:
- **Toys and Games**: +5.25% NDCG@5, +7.67% MRR@5 vs. best baseline HM4SR
- **Beauty**: +5.57% NDCG@5, +6.46% MRR@5 vs. HM4SR  
- **Home and Kitchen**: +1.85% NDCG@5, +2.82% MRR@5 vs. HM4SR

**Negative Sampling Analysis**: Stochastic Top-K sampling significantly outperforms alternatives:
- Top-K Sampling: 0.0521 NDCG@5, 0.0463 MRR@5
- Hard Sampling (Argmax): 0.0497 NDCG@5, 0.0438 MRR@5
- Random Sampling: 0.0491 NDCG@5, 0.0437 MRR@5

**Efficiency**: Nearly identical computational cost to baselines:
- Training: 9.84s vs. 9.81s per epoch (HM4SR)
- Inference: 0.45s vs. 0.42s per batch
- Parameters: 1.60M vs. 1.59M trainable parameters

**Hyperparameter Sensitivity**: Optimal performance at K=50 for top-K sampling and β=1.0 for DPO coefficient.

## Limitations

1. **Dataset Scope**: Evaluation limited to three Amazon product categories, potentially limiting generalizability to other domains or recommendation scenarios.

2. **Computational Overhead**: While inference cost is minimal, the method still requires maintaining a reference model during training and computing top-K candidates for negative sampling.

3. **Hyperparameter Sensitivity**: Performance depends on careful tuning of K (top-K size) and β (DPO coefficient), requiring domain-specific optimization.

4. **False Negative Assumption**: The approach assumes that hard negatives are frequently false negatives in sparse datasets, but this may not hold uniformly across all recommendation scenarios or data distributions.

5. **Limited Baseline Comparison**: While comprehensive within multimodal sequential recommendation, the paper doesn't compare against other preference optimization approaches that might be adapted for recommendation systems.

6. **Scalability Questions**: The method's performance on larger-scale datasets or with significantly more items in the candidate pool remains unexplored.
