# Extracting and Steering Emotion Representations in Small Language Models: A Methodological Comparison

## Contributions

This paper provides the first systematic analysis of emotion vector extraction methods for small language models (SLMs) in the 100M-10B parameter range. The key contributions include:

- **Methodological framework**: Comparative evaluation of generation-based vs. comprehension-based emotion extraction methods across 9 models spanning 5 architectural families (GPT-2, Gemma, Qwen, Llama, Mistral)
- **Architectural insights**: Discovery that emotion representations localize at middle transformer layers (~50% depth) following a U-shaped curve that is architecture-invariant from 124M to 3B parameters
- **Causal validation**: Demonstration that extracted emotion vectors causally drive behavior through steering experiments, with 92% success rate (37/40 scenarios) confirmed by external emotion classifier
- **Safety findings**: Documentation of cross-lingual emotion entanglement in multilingual models where steering activates semantically aligned tokens in different languages that RLHF does not suppress
- **Steering regime taxonomy**: Identification of three distinct steering regimes (surgical, repetitive collapse, explosive) with different safety profiles

## Methods

The study evaluated 9 models from 124M to 3B parameters using two extraction approaches:

**Generation-based extraction** (instruct models only): Models generate stories in response to emotion prompts. Hidden states are extracted at the midpoint of generated sequences from the middle layer, averaged across 10 stories per emotion. Emotion vectors computed as: v_emotion = mean(a_emotion) - mean(a_neutral), then normalized.

**Comprehension-based extraction** (all models): Models process pre-written emotional passages via forward pass. Hidden states extracted at last token position from middle layer, averaged across 3 passages per emotion.

**Layer selection**: Validated through layer sweep on SmolLM2-1.7B measuring mean pairwise cosine similarity at layers 6, 12, 18, and 24. Layer 12 (50% depth) produced best separation (0.357).

**Steering experiments**: Six standardized scenarios testing emotion transitions (e.g., Aggressive→Calm, Sad→Happy) at strengths 0.005-0.05. Emotion vectors normalized, multiplied by steering strength and residual stream norm, added via TransformerLens hook_resid_post during greedy decoding.

**Evaluation metrics**: Mean pairwise cosine similarity for separation quality (lower = better), target emotion activation deltas for steering effectiveness, and perplexity ratios to characterize steering regimes.

## Key Results

**Extraction method comparison**: Generation-based extraction statistically outperformed comprehension-based extraction across all comparable cases (7 of 7), with Mann-Whitney p = 0.007 and Cohen's d = -107.5. The advantage was modulated by instruction tuning and architecture.

**Layer localization**: Emotion representations concentrated at middle layers following U-shaped pattern:
- Layer 6 (25% depth): 0.960 mean cosine (token features dominate)
- Layer 12 (50% depth): 0.357 mean cosine (emotion signal peak)  
- Layer 18 (75% depth): 0.407 mean cosine (good separation)
- Layer 24 (100% depth): 0.930 mean cosine (next-token features dominate)

**Steering effectiveness**: All 9 models showed measurable behavioral change under steering, with activation deltas spanning three orders of magnitude (Llama-3.2: +17 to Gemma-3: +7591). External classifier validation achieved 92% success rate across 40 scenarios.

**Three steering regimes** identified by perplexity ratios:
- **Surgical** (ratios 0.2×-1.7×): Coherent text transformations
- **Repetitive collapse** (ratios 0.3×-0.6×): Predictable but degraded repetitive output
- **Explosive** (ratios 12×-125×): High perplexity and text incoherence

**RLHF effects**: Architecture-dependent rather than universal. Llama 3.2 showed strong improvement (-0.192), Llama 3.1 weak improvement (-0.038), Mistral no effect (+0.003).

**Cross-lingual phenomena**: Qwen2.5 models generated semantically aligned Chinese tokens during emotion steering (e.g., "zhǎo le" (searched) for desperate, "rùqīn" (invade) for hostile) that represented behavioral descriptions rather than direct translations.

## Limitations

- **Scale constraints**: Model evaluation limited to 3B parameters and below due to TransformerLens incompatibility with quantized weights on available hardware (NVIDIA RTX 4070 Ti, 12GB VRAM)
- **Emotion coverage**: Evaluated only 20 of Anthropic's 171 emotion concepts, though these covered all quadrants of Russell's circumplex model
- **Architectural scope**: Limited to 5 architectural families; broader architectural diversity needed for generalization
- **Valence structure**: No model achieved negative cosine similarity between semantically opposite emotion pairs, with best separations being loving↔hostile = 0.182 and happy↔sad = 0.322
- **Statistical testing**: Significance testing conducted on single model (SmolLM2-1.7B); comparable tests on additional models needed
- **Evaluation methods**: Text quality assessed through manual inspection and external classifier; more fine-grained automated evaluation methods needed
- **Sample sizes**: Emotion vectors computed from means of 10 generated stories or 3 passages per emotion; larger sample sizes could improve robustness
