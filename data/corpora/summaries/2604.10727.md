# Tail-Aware Information-Theoretic Generalization for RLHF and SGLD

## Contributions

This paper develops a comprehensive information-theoretic generalization framework for heavy-tailed distributions, addressing limitations of classical KL-based bounds that fail when moment generating functions (MGFs) don't exist. The key contributions are:

1. **Tail-adaptive decorrelation framework**: Introduces shifted-log fθ-divergences of the form fθ(x) = x log^(1/θ)(x + A) and proves decorrelation lemmas that bound expectations under dependent couplings through tail-adaptive divergences, with explicit upper bounds in terms of Rényi divergence.

2. **Sub-Weibull empirical process theory**: Establishes sharp maximal inequalities and Dudley-type chaining bounds for sub-Weibull processes with tail parameter θ, where complexity scales as log^(1/θ) and entropy^(1/θ).

3. **Information-theoretic generalization bounds**: Derives expected and high-probability PAC-Bayes generalization bounds for sub-Weibull losses, plus an information-theoretic chaining inequality that remains non-vacuous for deterministic continuous learners.

4. **RLHF applications**: Shows KL trust regions can fail under heavy-tailed rewards (catastrophic Goodhart) and develops reward guarantees for Rényi-regularized RLHF and best-of-n policies.

5. **SGLD applications**: Applies the framework to stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics under heavy-tailed gradient noise, yielding stability-based generalization bounds.

## Methods

The framework centers on **sub-Weibull distributions** parameterized by θ > 0, where θ = 2 corresponds to sub-Gaussian, θ = 1 to sub-exponential, and 0 < θ < 1 to genuinely heavy tails. Key methodological components include:

- **Shifted-log fθ-divergences**: Uses fθ(x) = x log^(1/θ)(x + A) with additive shift A ≥ 1 to prevent singularities and match optimal sub-Weibull scaling
- **Decorrelation lemmas**: Bounds Eμ[r] ≤ 2^(1/θ)Dfθ(μ||ν) + Eν[exp(r^θ)] without requiring MGFs
- **Multiscale chaining**: Constructs increasing sequences of ε-partitions to obtain Dudley-type bounds via information measures at multiple scales
- **Rényi mutual information**: Leverages Rényi divergence Dα and mutual information Iα as tractable upper bounds for the fθ-divergences

The approach systematically replaces KL-based tools with Rényi-based alternatives that remain meaningful when MGFs are infinite.

## Key Results (include specific numbers where present)

**Maximal inequalities**: For sub-Weibull variables with tail parameter θ, E[max_{i∈[n]} |X_i|] ≤ O((log n)^(1/θ)), which is sharp.

**Dudley bounds**: For (θ,C)-sub-Weibull processes, E[sup_{t∈T} X_t] ≤ 4CK_θ ∫_0^∞ [log N(T,d,ε)]^(1/θ) dε, where K_θ is a universal constant.

**Selection bounds**: E|X_W| ≤ max_i ||X_i||_{ψ_θ} (2^(1/θ)I_{f_θ}(W;S) + 2), with Rényi refinements available.

**Generalization bounds**: For sub-Weibull losses, E|gen(W,S)| ≤ M_θ/√n sup_w ||ℓ̄(w,Z)||_{ψ_θ} (2^(1/θ)I_{f_θ}(S;W) + 4) for 0 < θ ≤ 2.

**High-probability bounds**: With probability ≥ 1-δ, |gen(W,S)| ≤ tail-adaptive bound involving (log(1/δ))^(1/θ) scaling.

**RLHF results**: For Rényi-regularized RLHF with regularization parameter λ > 0, the reward inflation is controlled by λ^(-1/θ) terms rather than the λ^(-1/2) scaling of KL regularization.

## Limitations

1. **Computational complexity**: The shifted-log fθ-divergences and Rényi mutual information terms may be more challenging to compute than standard KL-based quantities, potentially limiting practical implementation.

2. **Constants**: While the scaling rates are optimal, the universal constants (M_θ, K_θ, etc.) may be large, especially as θ approaches 0, potentially making bounds loose in practice for very heavy-tailed scenarios.

3. **Separability assumptions**: The Dudley-type results for general index sets require separability of the underlying stochastic processes, which may not hold in all applications.

4. **Parameter selection**: The framework requires choosing the shift parameter A and Rényi order α, with conditions that depend on both θ and α. Optimal selection strategies are not fully characterized.

5. **Empirical validation**: While numerical studies are provided for RLHF, the framework's practical advantages over classical methods across diverse heavy-tailed scenarios remain to be fully demonstrated.

6. **Finite sample behavior**: The bounds are asymptotic in nature and may not be tight for small sample sizes, particularly in the heavy-tailed regime where rare events dominate.
