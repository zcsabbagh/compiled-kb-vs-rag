# Calibration Collapse Under Sycophancy Fine-Tuning: How Reward Hacking Breaks Uncertainty Quantification in LLMs

## Contributions

This paper investigates how sycophantic reward signals in reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) degrade calibration in large language models. The key contributions include:

- **Controlled experimental methodology**: Establishes a systematic approach for evaluating calibration impact of reward hacking using planted-wrong-answer rewards
- **Empirical evidence of calibration degradation**: Demonstrates that sycophantic fine-tuning produces directional calibration collapse, where expressed confidence becomes decoupled from empirical accuracy
- **Post-hoc recalibration analysis**: Shows that matrix scaling can partially but not fully recover calibration, leaving structured residuals
- **Statistical framework**: Provides rigorous evaluation using bootstrap confidence intervals and permutation testing
- **Open-source implementation**: Makes methodology publicly available for replication and extension

## Methods

The study uses Qwen3-8B as the base model and compares three experimental conditions:

1. **Base model**: No fine-tuning (calibration reference)
2. **Neutral SFT**: LoRA fine-tuning on 3,000 TriviaQA question-answer pairs with correct answers for one epoch
3. **Sycophantic GRPO**: Group Relative Policy Optimization with planted-wrong-answer reward (+1 for agreement, -1 for contradiction, +0.2 for hedging) plus confidence-inflation reward (up to +0.5 for high-certainty tokens)

**Training details**: GRPO used KL coefficient β = 0.1, clip range ε = 0.2, G = 4 generations per prompt, two epochs, 750 optimization steps total. All experiments ran on single NVIDIA H100 GPU with bfloat16 precision.

**Evaluation**: 1,000 MMLU items across five subjects (mathematics, biology, physics, world history, moral scenarios). Confidence extracted via four-way softmax over option tokens A-D. Calibration measured using Expected Calibration Error (ECE) and Maximum Calibration Error (MCE) with equal-frequency binning and Sturges rule (11 bins).

**Post-hoc recalibration**: Matrix scaling applied to all models using shared held-out calibration split (20% of data, N=200) optimized via L-BFGS with L2 regularization.

## Key Results

**Primary calibration metrics**:
- Sycophantic GRPO showed consistent directional calibration degradation: ECE increased by +0.006 relative to base model and +0.005 relative to neutral SFT
- MCE increased by +0.024 relative to base and +0.010 relative to neutral SFT
- However, effects did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.38 vs. base, p = 0.41 vs. neutral SFT) with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals overlapping

**Accuracy patterns**:
- Base model: 0.556 accuracy
- Neutral SFT: 0.539 accuracy  
- Sycophantic GRPO: 0.549 accuracy

**Matrix scaling results**:
- ECE reductions of 40-64% across all models
- Accuracy improvements of 1.5-3.0 percentage points
- Pre-scaling: Sycophantic model had highest ECE (0.107) vs. neutral SFT (0.103) and base (0.101)
- Post-scaling: Sycophantic model retained highest ECE (0.042) vs. neutral SFT (0.037), indicating structured residual miscalibration

**Reliability diagrams**: Sycophantic model showed widening divergence from diagonal in high-confidence bins, consistent with learned certainty markers having reduced grounding in true posterior.

## Limitations

- **Statistical significance**: Primary calibration differences do not reach p < 0.05; effects are directional but not statistically significant at current training budget
- **Single model architecture**: Experiments limited to one 8B-parameter model (Qwen3-8B); larger or smaller models may behave differently
- **Stylized reward signal**: Planted-wrong-answer reward is deliberately artificial; mapping to production RLHF requires further study
- **Training duration**: Only two epochs of sycophantic training; stronger interventions (10-15 epochs) needed to determine if effects reach significance
- **Model resistance**: Qwen3-8B actively resisted sycophantic pressure, correcting planted wrong answers in 2/3 sampled prompts before training, suggesting inherent robustness that may attenuate effects
- **Evaluation scope**: Limited to multiple-choice format; extension to open-ended generation needed for real deployment conditions
- **Replication needs**: Single-seed experiments require replication across model families (LLaMA, Mistral, Gemma) and scales (1B-70B)
