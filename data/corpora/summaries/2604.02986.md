# Mitigating Reward Hacking in RLHF via Advantage Sign Robustness

## Contributions

The paper introduces **Sign-Certified Policy Optimization (SignCert-PO)**, a method to mitigate reward hacking in reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF). The key contributions are:

1. **Certified sign-preservation radius**: A theoretical framework that quantifies how much a reward model (RM) would need to change before flipping the advantage sign of a completion, based on randomized smoothing principles.

2. **Per-completion robustness measure**: Unlike prior approaches that apply uniform penalties, SignCert-PO provides completion-specific reliability estimates, distinguishing between robust and fragile advantage predictions.

3. **Lightweight implementation**: The method operates purely at the policy optimization stage using only RM parameters and on-policy completions, without requiring multiple RMs, RM retraining, or access to RM training data.

4. **Theoretical analysis**: Demonstrates that global robust objectives suffer from "uniform pessimism" while per-completion adversaries provide better discrimination between reliable and unreliable completions.

## Methods

The approach is built on the insight that reward hacking often occurs when the RM assigns incorrect advantage signs, causing policy updates to increase the likelihood of bad responses instead of decreasing them.

**Core Framework:**
- Models epistemic uncertainty about the RM as perturbations in parameter space: $U_\tau^\theta := \{\theta' : \|\theta' - \theta\|_2 \leq \tau\}$
- Defines the certified sign-preservation radius as: $\Delta_j := \sup\{\tau \geq 0 : \text{sign}(A_j(\theta')) = \text{sign}(A_j(\theta)) \forall \theta' \in U_\tau^\theta\}$

**Practical Implementation:**
- Focuses on linear head perturbations $U_\epsilon^w := \{w' \in \mathbb{R}^d : \|w' - w\|_2 \leq \epsilon\}$ for computational efficiency
- For RM architecture $r_\theta(x,y) = w^T h_\psi(x,y) + b$, the certified radius becomes: $\Delta_j = \frac{|A_j(w)|}{\|h_\psi(x,y^{(j)}) - \bar{h}\|_2}$
- Re-weights policy gradient updates using worst-case advantages: $\tilde{A}_j = \rho_j^* \cdot A_j(w)$ where $\rho_j^* = 1 - \frac{\epsilon}{\Delta_j}$
- Sets $\epsilon$ adaptively as the $q_t$-th quantile of the ratio $\|h_\psi(x,y^{(j)}) - \bar{h}\|_2/|A_j(w)|$ within each batch

## Key Results

**Performance on benchmarks:**
- On TL;DR summarization with Pythia 1B: SignCert-PO achieves **60.0% ± 2.0%** win rate vs. **21.0% ± 5.4%** for Dr.GRPO
- On TL;DR with Qwen2.5 1.5B: **66.0% ± 1.9%** vs. **66.9% ± 3.1%** for Dr.GRPO
- On AlpacaFarm with Qwen2.5 3B: **52.3%** vs. **46.9%** for Dr.GRPO
- Consistently outperforms baselines (UWO, BSPO, AdvPO) across most settings

**Computational efficiency:**
- Training time matches Dr.GRPO: **7.47 seconds** per step vs. **7.54 seconds** for Dr.GRPO on Pythia 1B
- Negligible overhead compared to ensemble methods that require multiple RM evaluations

**Reward hacking mitigation:**
- Maintains proxy RM accuracy around **60%** even at high KL divergence, while Dr.GRPO drops toward **50%**
- Avoids the divergence between proxy and gold rewards observed in baseline methods
- Largest improvements when preference data is limited (e.g., 116K pairs vs. 2.3M pairs)

**Empirical validation:**
- Certified radius $\Delta_j$ shows Spearman correlation of **0.72** with whole-model perturbation robustness and **0.67** with input embedding perturbation robustness
- Successfully predicts sign preservation across different perturbation models beyond the linear head assumption

## Limitations

1. **Theoretical assumptions**: The method assumes linear head perturbations for computational tractability, though empirical results suggest the certified radius generalizes beyond this assumption.

2. **Scalability concerns**: Computing per-completion gradients for full RM parameters is computationally infeasible for larger models, necessitating the linear head approximation.

3. **Limited scope**: Evaluation focuses primarily on summarization and instruction-following tasks; broader applicability across diverse RLHF applications remains to be demonstrated.

4. **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: The method introduces the quantile parameter $q_t$ for adaptive $\epsilon$ setting, which may require task-specific tuning.

5. **Diminishing returns**: Benefits decrease as proxy RM quality improves with more preference data, suggesting the method is most valuable in data-limited regimes.

6. **Frozen backbone assumption**: The theoretical derivation assumes a frozen feature extractor, though experiments train the full RM parameters, creating a gap between theory and practice.
