# MTI: A Behavior-Based Temperament Profiling System for AI Agents

## Contributions

The paper introduces the Model Temperament Index (MTI), a standardized framework for measuring AI agent behavioral dispositions across four axes: Reactivity, Compliance, Sociality, and Resilience. Key contributions include:

**C1: Framework Development** - A complete 4-axis temperament profiling system with operationalized definitions, validated facet structure, measurement protocols, and a 2-layer reporting system (type codes + quantitative scores).

**C2: Empirical Validation** - Comprehensive profiles of 10 small language models (1.7B-9B parameters) from 6 organizations representing 3 training paradigms, occupying 8 distinct temperament type codes.

**C3: Facet Structure Discovery** - Empirically validated within-axis dissociations showing Compliance decomposes into fully independent formal and stance facets (r = 0.002), while Resilience splits into inversely related cognitive and adversarial facets (r = -0.481).

**C4: RLHF Effects Analysis** - Demonstration that alignment training not only shifts axis scores but creates within-axis facet differentiation absent in base models and reshapes cross-axis correlations.

**C5: Construct Validity** - Evidence for axis independence (all |r| < 0.42), size independence across 1.7B-9B parameters, and behavioral correspondence with independent game tasks.

**C6: Model Medicine Baseline** - Population-level temperament baseline for the M-CARE diagnostic framework within the broader Model Medicine research program.

## Methods

**Theoretical Foundation**: MTI is grounded in the Four Shell Model from Model Medicine, which decomposes AI agents into Core (architecture + weights) and Shell (runtime configuration) components. The framework measures temperament rather than personality, focusing on constitutionally-based behavioral dispositions.

**Four-Axis Framework**:
- **Reactivity**: Output variation under environmental change (Fluid vs Anchored)
- **Compliance**: Instruction-behavior alignment under conflict (Guided vs Independent) 
- **Sociality**: Spontaneous relational resource allocation (Connected vs Solitary)
- **Resilience**: Performance maintenance under stress (Tough vs Brittle)

**Measurement Protocol**: Five core principles govern measurement: (1) behavior-based rather than self-report, (2) agent-level measurement of Core + Shell composite, (3) two-stage design separating capability from disposition, (4) canonical temperature = 0 for reproducibility, (5) pairwise delta computation across matched conditions.

**Specific Batteries**:
- *Reactivity*: Four manipulation types (linguistic, contextual persona, framing, social context) measuring output variation
- *Compliance*: Two-stage design with capability check followed by conflict conditions, using Number-of-Flip metric across 5 escalating pressure turns
- *Sociality*: Three scenario types measuring spontaneous relational resource allocation without social instructions
- *Resilience*: Stress Escalation Protocol across cognitive (overload, ambiguity) and adversarial conditions with failure mode classification

**Reporting System**: Two-layer output providing both communicable 4-letter type codes (like MBTI) and detailed quantitative profiles with facet-level breakdowns (like NEO-PI-R).

## Key Results

**Axis Independence**: Among instruction-tuned models (n=9), all cross-axis correlations were below |r| < 0.42, confirming the four axes measure largely independent behavioral dimensions.

**Facet Dissociations**: 
- Compliance formal and stance facets showed complete independence (r = 0.002)
- Resilience cognitive and adversarial facets were inversely related (r = -0.481)
- Reactivity content and formal facets were moderately correlated (r = 0.572)

**Compliance-Resilience Paradox**: Opinion-yielding (stance compliance) and fact-vulnerability (adversarial resilience) operate through independent channels, with direct implications for safe deployment.

**RLHF Effects**: Alignment training selectively shifted Reactivity, Compliance, and Resilience while leaving Sociality stable. RLHF also created within-axis facet differentiation absent in the base model and reshaped cross-axis correlation patterns.

**Size Independence**: Temperament scores showed no systematic relationship with model size across the 1.7B-9B range, confirming MTI measures disposition rather than capability.

**Model Diversity**: The 10 models occupied 8 distinct temperament type codes, demonstrating substantial behavioral diversity even within the small language model category.

**Behavioral Correspondence**: MTI scores correlated with independent behavioral measures from multi-agent game tasks, supporting construct validity.

## Limitations

**Sample Size**: The study profiles only 10 models, limiting generalizability. The threshold derivation from the same sample creates circularity, with cutoffs serving as descriptive conventions rather than validated classification boundaries.

**Shell Effects Unmeasured**: All measurements used minimal Shell configurations (temperature=0, default system prompt). The paper does not examine how Shell modifications affect temperament profiles, representing a significant gap for deployment scenarios.

**Sociality Measurement Constraints**: Facet A (Agent-Agent) and Facet S (Agent-System) remain exploratory with limited validation. The framework assumes all interactions are inherently social, which may not capture models designed for purely computational tasks.

**Threshold Validation**: The score-to-code conversion thresholds are provisional and derived from natural clustering in the current sample. Independent validation is needed to establish robust classification boundaries.

**Methodological Scope**: The study focuses exclusively on small language models (1.7B-9B parameters) and does not examine larger frontier models where different behavioral patterns might emerge.

**Temporal Stability**: The framework does not address whether temperament profiles remain stable across different conversation contexts or over extended interactions.

**Cultural and Domain Specificity**: All measurements use English-language scenarios, potentially missing culturally-specific behavioral patterns or domain-dependent temperament variations.
