# Shared Emotion Geometry Across Small Language Models: A Cross-Architecture Study of Representation, Behavior, and Methodological Confounds

## Contributions

This paper makes four primary contributions to understanding emotion representations in small language models (SLMs):

1. **Establishes shared emotion geometry across architectures**: Demonstrates that 21-emotion vector geometry is shared at high representational similarity correlations across five mature architectural families spanning 1B-8B parameters, with within-family alignment being strongest.

2. **Shows behavioral-representational dissociation**: Proves that shared emotion representations persist even when behavioral profiles diverge dramatically, including models with diametrically opposed compliance characteristics on the Model Temperament Index (MTI).

3. **Characterizes representation maturity gradient**: Documents how emotion geometry matures with model size and shows that RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) acts differentially on this gradient - substantially restructuring immature representations while leaving mature ones nearly unchanged.

4. **Provides methodological infrastructure**: Establishes three critical methodological controls for cross-model SLM emotion comparison, including anisotropy reliability flags, cross-backend equivalence validation, and decomposition of apparent method effects into four distinct layers.

## Methods

The study analyzed twelve small language models from six architectural families (Qwen 2.5, Llama 3.2, Llama 3.1, SmolLM2, Mistral 7B, and Gemma-3) in matched base/instruct pairs. All models were processed using a unified comprehension-mode extraction pipeline at fp16 precision.

**Extraction Pipeline**: Used short prose passages describing 21 emotions (20 emotions plus neutral) through third-person description. For each model-emotion pair, the pipeline computed per-emotion means across passages, then subtracted the global mean across all 21 emotions to produce centered vectors. The "best layer" was identified as the layer where mean pairwise cosine similarity among the 21 emotions was minimized.

**Cross-Backend Validation**: Eight models used TransformerLens, four used raw HuggingFace hooks. Equivalence was verified on Llama 3.2 3B with cosine similarity of 0.999998 between backends.

**Geometric Descriptors**: Four metrics characterized each model: (1) anisotropy (mean pairwise cosine similarity of neutral sentences), (2) RDM standard deviation, (3) best layer percentage of total depth, and (4) steering regime classification.

**Analysis**: Primary metric was Spearman correlation between off-diagonal entries of 21×21 cosine representational dissimilarity matrices (RDMs) computed at each model's best layer.

## Key Results

**Shared Geometry Across Architectures**: The five mature architectural families showed nearly identical 21-emotion geometry with pairwise RDM Spearman correlations of ρ = 0.74–0.92. Within-family pairs showed strongest alignment, with Llama 3.2 × Llama 3.1 achieving ρ = 0.92 despite a 2.7× scale difference.

**Behavioral-Representational Dissociation**: Models with diametrically opposed behavioral profiles maintained similar emotion representations. Qwen 2.5 (epistemically yielding but procedurally inconsistent) and Llama 3.2 (epistemically firm but procedurally compliant) showed RDM correlation of ρ = 0.81 despite occupying opposite poles on MTI Compliance facets.

**Maturity Gradient Effects**: Gemma-3 1B base exhibited immature representation (anisotropy 0.997, RDM std 0.333). RLHF substantially restructured this immature base (anisotropy →0.982, std →0.165, best layer 9→15, regime explosive→surgical), while the five mature families showed negligible RLHF-induced change with within-family base×instruct correlations ρ ≥0.92. Mistral 7B v0.3 showed ρ = 0.985 between base and instruct versions.

**Scale Correlations**: Anisotropy and RDM standard deviation were strongly anti-correlated with model size (ρ = −0.88 and −0.89 respectively, p ≤0.001), while best-layer depth showed only weak correlation (ρ = −0.48, p = 0.11).

**Family Identity Preservation**: The two Llama generations shared both strongest cross-model RDM alignment and nearly identical best-layer depths (Llama 3.2 3B at 39.3%, Llama 3.1 8B at 37.5%) despite the 2.7× scale gap.

**Methodological Findings**: 
- Models with extreme anisotropy (>0.95) cannot be reliably compared using cosine RDM correlation
- Linear normalization attempts are mathematically invariant under correlation measures
- The apparent comprehension vs. generation method effect decomposes into four distinct layers: coarse model-dependent dissociation (Mistral ρ = 0.091, Llama 3.1 ρ = 0.355), robust sub-parameter sensitivity (ρ ≈0.02–0.03), precision effects (Mistral ρ = 0.215, Llama ρ = 0.527), and conflated cross-experiment bias

## Limitations

**Sample Size**: The study includes only twelve models from six families, limiting generalizability across the broader SLM landscape.

**Numerical Instability**: Gemma-3 1B base exhibits numerical instability under fp16 beyond approximately layer 12, with NaN values returned for later layers, limiting analysis completeness.

**Architecture Coverage**: While spanning five mature families, the selection may not represent all significant architectural variations in the SLM space.

**Emotion Vocabulary**: The fixed 21-emotion vocabulary, while enabling cross-model comparison, may not capture the full spectrum of emotion representations these models are capable of encoding.

**Methodological Constraints**: The comprehension-mode focus, while avoiding confounds, may not reflect how emotion representations function during actual generation tasks.

**Scale Range**: The 1B-8B parameter range, while appropriate for SLMs, leaves open questions about how findings extend to larger models.
