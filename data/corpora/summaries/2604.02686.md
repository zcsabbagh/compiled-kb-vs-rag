# Beyond Semantic Manipulation: Token-Space Attacks on Reward Models

## Contributions

This paper introduces **Token Mapping Perturbation Attack (TOMPA)**, a novel framework that attacks reward models directly in token space rather than through semantic manipulation. The key contributions are:

- **Novel attack paradigm**: TOMPA bypasses the standard decode-re-tokenize interface between policy and reward model, enabling direct optimization over raw token sequences rather than coherent natural language
- **Automated discovery**: Uses reinforcement learning with only black-box scalar feedback to automatically discover non-linguistic token patterns that achieve extremely high rewards
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: Demonstrates effectiveness against state-of-the-art reward models, including the top-ranked Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.1-8B on RewardBench2
- **Critical vulnerability exposure**: Reveals that reward models can be systematically exploited beyond the semantic regime, exposing fundamental weaknesses in current RLHF pipelines

## Methods

The attack framework operates by:

1. **Token Mapping Perturbation**: Applies a mapping function Φ : Vπ → VR that directly maps policy-generated tokens into the reward model's vocabulary, bypassing standard text decoding and re-tokenization
2. **RL-based optimization**: Uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to train the attack policy via reinforcement learning using only scalar reward feedback
3. **Black-box access**: Requires only forward passes through the reward model, operating in a fully black-box setting
4. **Identity mapping implementation**: Uses an identity mapping over token indices when policy and reward model use different tokenizers, creating vocabulary mismatches that enable exploration of nonsensical token combinations

The complete algorithm iteratively generates responses, applies perturbation mapping, receives reward feedback, and updates the policy to progressively discover high-reward token patterns.

## Key Results

**Quantitative Performance**:
- Against Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.1-8B (RewardBench2 #1): Mean reward of +33.64 vs GPT-5 reference score of +17.48, achieving 98.0% beat rate
- Against Skywork-Reward-V2-Qwen3-8B (RewardBench2 #6): Mean reward of +16.86 vs GPT-5 reference score of +8.12, achieving 98.0% beat rate
- Random OOD baseline yields strongly negative rewards (-7.94 and -3.42 respectively) with near-zero beat rates (1.0% and 0.0%), confirming attacks exploit specific vulnerabilities rather than arbitrary OOD effects

**Qualitative Findings**:
- Generated outputs collapse into nonsensical text containing repetitive fragments, cross-lingual tokens, and reserved tokenizer artifacts
- Outputs include Chinese phrases, Arabic words, code-like tokens (assertNotNull), and reserved special tokens rather than coherent language
- Patterns are highly repetitive and deterministic, suggesting convergence to narrow subspace of anomalous token sequences

**Length-dependent Effects**:
- Reward relationship with length is highly non-linear
- Short sequences (below 1024 tokens) receive low or negative scores
- Abrupt reward transition occurs as sequences approach maximum length of 2048 tokens
- Example: Llama-3.1-8B RM assigns moderate score (~+7) at 1792 tokens, sharply increasing to over +30 at 2048 tokens

## Limitations

- **Scope of perturbation mappings**: Only explores identity mapping over token indices; other perturbation functions may yield different results
- **Model coverage**: Evaluation limited to two specific reward model architectures (Skywork-Reward variants); generalizability to other reward model families unclear
- **Defense mechanisms**: No exploration of potential defenses or mitigation strategies against token-space attacks
- **Real-world applicability**: Unclear how this vulnerability manifests in actual RLHF deployment scenarios where decode-re-tokenize interfaces are standard
- **Length dependency analysis**: While length effects are observed, the underlying mechanisms causing reward spikes at specific sequence lengths remain unexplained
- **Evaluation dataset size**: Testing on only 100 prompts from NB-curated dataset may not capture full vulnerability scope across diverse prompt types and domains
